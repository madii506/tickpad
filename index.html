How it lives
ONE HOST · ONE LIFE · ONE PAYOUT

    

      

        
A tick attaches to one stock at launch and can never be moved to another. The coin's creator fee is wired to its own wallet at birth — not the dev's, not ours.

        
It costs one cent a minute to stay attached. Its clock is that balance in seconds. Thirty days is all the life it will hold.

        
Everything above thirty days it spends on its host, once an hour, and posts the fill. It never sells. There is no sell function.

        
At 00:00:00 it lets go: every share it ever bought goes to the holders at that block, and it is finished. It does not attach again.

This is the only address on the site. Every tick gets its own wallet at birth, deployed in the same transaction as its coin, and the coin's creator fee is wired straight into it — not to the dev, not here.

        
The key that operates a tick's wallet can do exactly two things: buy the host, and distribute on death. It cannot sell, cannot transfer out, cannot be paused and cannot be pointed at a different stock. There is no upgrade path and no admin key behind it.

        
None of that has to be taken on faith. The buy and the release are both transactions, so every one of them is a hash you can open. The hourly schedule is the single thing you trust until the keeper is on chain, and the log on each tick is how you check it.

      

    

  






  

    
The rules, frozen
NINE LINES, NONE OF THEM OPTIONAL

    
    
      
  1. One host, named at launch, fixed for the whole life. It cannot be changed by you, by us, or by a vote.
    2. 
      
  2. The coin's creator fee is wired to the tick's own wallet at birth. Not to the dev's wallet. Not to ours.
    3. 
      
  3. One cent a minute to stay attached. The rate is in the contract, not in a setting.
    4. 
      
  4. Thirty days is the most clock a tick can hold. It will not hoard more time than that.
    5. 
      
  5. Everything above thirty days buys the host, once an hour, with a minimum received and a dry run first.
    6. 
      
  6. Every fill is posted: the price, the size, and the hash.
    7. 
      
  7. There is no sell function. A share it has bought, it has bought for good.
    8. 
      
  8. At 00:00:00 the entire position goes to the holders of its coin, pro rata, in that same hour. A transfer, not a claim.
    9. 
      
  9. A tick that has let go never attaches again. One life, one payout.

The pad keeps the launch fee, printed on the launch page before you sign, and takes nothing from any tick at any point after that.

The hard questions
ASKED BEFORE YOU ASK THEM

    

      
Why launch here instead of anywhere else?
Because your coin comes out of the gate attached to a real asset that only accumulates, and the fees you would have pocketed turn into a position your holders eventually own outright.

      
Why would anyone hold?
Because the release goes to whoever holds at the block it happens, and nobody — not the dev, not the pad — can tell you in advance when that block is.

      
So everyone will just try to starve it.
Some will, and that is playing the game correctly. It is a public rule with a public clock, and the tick says exactly how much time it has left the entire way down.

      
What if the dev dumps?
The dev never had the fees to dump. Selling shortens the clock, which brings the payout closer for everyone still holding — including the people he sold to.

      
Can you stop a tick?
No. We cannot touch its wallet, and it cannot touch anything but its host. Only silence stops it.

      
What happens if the host is not tokenized yet?
It waits, holding the cash and buying nothing, and it says so on its own page rather than quietly buying something else.

    

  






  

    
What is not built yet
THE HONEST LIST

    

      

        
No ticks exist. The first one is born when the first coin launches, and the four numbers above stay at zero until then — a dash is not a zero and a zero is not a guess.

        
The keeper that runs the hour is not on chain yet. The board you can play with is a rehearsal running in your own browser against the real prices of the day; clear it whenever you like with Reset board.

      

      

        
What is real on this page: the prices, read from the market; the wallet address and its balance, read on the date shown; and the rules, which are the same ones that go into the contract.

        
What is not: every tick on the board, its balance, its fills and its release. Those are there so you can see the shape of the thing before you put money near it.
